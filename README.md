## Projects

### Vibration Testing Jig
**Euclid Techlabs · Summer 2026**

After an Ultra Fast Pulser (UFP) was damaged during shipping, I worked on
evaluating its mounting system to help prevent another failure. I designed
and built a testing jig, tested the system at six excitation frequencies,
and used FFT analysis to compare its vibration response.

The tests showed amplification ranging from 1.12× to 8×. I used those
results to recommend changes to the damping configuration. Wire rope
isolators helped improve the system, although that improvement was not
quantified.

### Copper Coating on Ferroelectric Ceramic
**Euclid Techlabs · Summer 2026**

I worked on bringing a ceramic metallization process in-house so the
company could prepare ferroelectric tubes for soldering without outsourcing
the copper coating.

The process had two main stages: sputtering thin titanium and copper layers
onto the ceramic, then electroplating a thicker copper layer. I measured
sputtering rates and tested different plating conditions, troubleshooting
burning, peeling, and poor adhesion along the way.

A slower current ramp and a revised holding fixture produced a 20 µm copper
coating with good adhesion and no burning in the successful trial. The
process will be used on future company projects.

### Suspension Damper Mount
**Terps Racing · Formula SAE**

I designed a damper mount for our Formula SAE car in SolidWorks and used
FEA to check its strength. The final design reduced the part’s weight by
73% while supporting a load exceeding 1,160 lb with a safety factor of 3.14.

I also reviewed the design with a machinist and made changes so it could
be CNC milled from a block of 7075-T6 Aluminum Alloy. This gave me experience balancing
weight and strength with how a part would actually be manufactured.

### I-Beam Design Competition
**University of Maryland**

I led a five-person team in designing and building a 20-inch pine I-beam.
The challenge was to maximize its load-to-weight ratio while designing it
to fail within a specified region.

Our beam held 1,507 lb before failing due to glue shear and placed first
among 11 teams.
